## Yakshas, the shadow of earth, and Vaishravana
[Original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2085012773709467773) (quotes [the Aug 5 post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2084803129758068912))

Having a lot of earth in your saju means you may easily be caught by the Five Yellow Killing, and rough yaksha-like impulses may rise up easily.

People with earth energy have a strong sense of responsibility and tend to suppress their feelings, and this turns into a shadow, and that shadow becomes yaksha-like impulsiveness.

In traditional Buddhist tales, "yakshas (夜叉)" are described as rough, fierce spirits dwelling in forests and nature, or barbaric demons living on human blood and flesh.

But when they come under the command of Vaishravana (the Heavenly King Who Hears Much), guardian of the north and god of wealth, they transform into fierce "Dharma-protecting divine generals (호법신장 護法神將)" who protect the Buddha's teaching and bring practitioners enormous wealth (富) and achievement.

Because of social face and morality, modern people suppress into the unconscious their blatant desire for worldly wealth (富), their aggression, and their anger. This repressed libido rots in the unconscious and grows into a destructive shadow called the "yaksha," ruining my plans at decisive moments or causing lethargy.

Vaishravana, as king of the yakshas, turns this rough energy into vitality and transforms it into a Dharma-protecting general who guards the wealth of my life.

Vaishravana mantra: Om Beisira Mandaya Sowaka

## Vaishravana's mantra as psycho-physiological alchemy
[Original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2084908221517730255) (quotes [the Aug 5 post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2084897938082508974))

The primordial energy stored in the body's kidneys and adrenal glands (jing, 精) is a volatile raw material, easily oxidized by stress (cortisol) or used up through physical discharge (ejaculation).

These are "pregnenolone," synthesized from cholesterol.

From the viewpoint of alchemy, how this raw material
is distributed decides life and death (生死) and success and failure.

When this energy loses its way, it's wasted
in the neurosis of anxiety (cortisol) or blind instinct (ejaculation).

From Carl Jung's viewpoint, this is the phenomenon of energy being devoured by the "Shadow" of the unconscious.

Reciting "Om Beisira Mandaya Sowaka" is

psycho-physiological alchemy that integrates
this primal libido into the archetypes of
the "Warrior," who makes you fight healthy battles,
and the "King," who gives birth to abundance.

Practice-phonetic analysis of Om Beisira Mandaya Sowaka →

B (*bei*, *ba*): striking the adrenals and tailbone. The plosive "b" strongly stimulates the body's deepest roots, the kidneys and coccyx (tailbone). This acts as a spark plug, waking the raw material (jing) sleeping in the adrenals and using it as motive power.

S (*si*): accelerating energy through friction. The fricative "s" adds sharp heat to the drawn-up qi, transforming heavy, turbid energy into a light, sharp state of sympathetic arousal (adrenaline).

R (*ra*): completing the microcosmic orbit (소주천 小周天). If this boiled-up adrenergic energy stays in the lower dantian, it degenerates into sexual desire, or gets blocked in the chest and becomes *hwabyeong* (pent-up anger illness, cortisol).

But the strong vibration of "r" rides the spine (the Governing Vessel) and spins this qi forcefully up to the brain (upper dantian), completing the microcosmic orbit.
