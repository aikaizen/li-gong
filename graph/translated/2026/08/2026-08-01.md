## The White-Robed dharani as a bio-control cycle

The core of the White-Robed Avalokiteshvara dharani is
"Tadyatha Om Garyabada Garyabada Gahabada
Ragyabada Ragyabada Svaha."

It isn't simple repetition; as the order of the consonants changes subtly (garya → gaha → raga), the paths by which it strikes the cranial nerves and spinal cord are elaborately designed.

[Tadyatha Om]
Da (d) – nya (n) – ta (t): warming up the brain and securing time and space

1. [Garyabada ×2]: descending from the brain down the spine, it washes away the body's sick energy like a washing machine.
2. [Gahabada ×1]: it injects the spiritual awakening of "the joy of suchness (h)" into the cleaned ventricles of the brain.
3. [Ragabada ×2]: it pumps that awakened energy powerfully from the bottom of the spine to the brain, amplifying it.
4. [Svaha]: it closes the perineum (s) and shoots the energy up to the center of the brain (h), storing it permanently.

In my experience, the highlight is the shift garya → gaha → raga:
garya, top-down purification from the brain to the lower dantian;
gaha, securing the thalamus's place of no-thought in the brain with the "h";
raga, energy circulating from the body to the brain, I think.

That's the practice reason why reciting the White-Robed Avalokiteshvara dharani brings water rising and fire descending (수승화강) and harmonizes the autonomic nervous system.

## The White-Robed Avalokiteshvara dharani, line by line
[Original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2083390175447769477)

The White-Robed Avalokiteshvara dharani (白衣觀音神呪)

Namo Great Love, Great Compassion, Saving from Suffering and Hardship, Vast Spiritual Response, Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva Mahasattva
Namo Buddha, Namo Dharma, Namo Sangha, Namo Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva, Saving from Suffering and Hardship

Tadyatha Om Garabada Garabada
Gahabada Ragabada Ragabada Svaha (×9)

Cheonrasin Jirasin Inrinan Nanrisin
Ilche Jaeang Hwawijin

Namo Maha Banya Baramil

1. Refuge and praise (relying on the Buddhas and bodhisattvas)

- Namo Great Love, Great Compassion, Saving from Suffering and Hardship, Vast Spiritual Response, Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva Mahasattva

An introduction expressing the resolve to lower oneself and rely wholly on the Three Jewels at Buddhism's core (the Buddha, the teaching, the monks) and on Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva.

- Namo Buddha, Namo Dharma, Namo Sangha, Namo Avalokiteshvara Bodhisattva, Saving from Suffering and Hardship

Buddha, Dharma, Sangha: taking refuge in the Three Jewels, Buddhism's three treasures (the Buddha, the Buddha's teaching, the community of monks).

2. The main dharani (proclaiming the mystic mantra)

This part transliterates Sanskrit into Chinese characters by sound; it's the core incantation, recited without translating the meaning, trusting the wave and mystic power of the sound itself.

Tadyatha Om Garabada Garabada Gahabada Ragabada Ragabada Svaha

3. Protection by the Dharma-guarding generals and extinguishing calamity (overcoming real-world hardship)

A powerful declaration that, through the power of Avalokiteshvara and the help of the generals (protective gods) of heaven and earth, all real-world calamities will be completely eliminated.

- Cheonrasin Jirasin Inrinan Nanrisin Ilche Jaeang Hwawijin

Cheonrasin (天羅神), Jirasin (地羅神): the gods of heaven and earth who protect as closely as heaven's net.
Inrinan (人離難), Nanrisin (難離神): gods who free people from the disasters arising among them and all kinds of tangled troubles.
Ilche jaeang hwawijin (一切災殃化爲塵): all calamities and hardships "turn to dust (化爲塵) and blow away." The most essential phrase for extinguishing bad luck.

4. Refuge in ultimate wisdom (conclusion toward awakening)

Going beyond the fortune-seeking level of simply avoiding real-world calamity, it ends with the resolve to attain perfect wisdom (awakening), the ultimate goal of Buddhism.

Namo Maha Banya Baramil
Maha: great, magnificent.
Banya (Prajna): wisdom that sees through the world as it is.
Baramil (Paramita): crossing to the other shore (the world of awakening), perfecting.
Interpretation: "Taking refuge in great and perfect wisdom, I will attain awakening."

- Naesosa Temple, painting of White-Robed Avalokiteshvara

## Ksitigarbha mantra practice (Ksitigarbha Reiki)
[Original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2083370809901625521)

Ksitigarbha mantra (Ksitigarbha Reiki)

Step 1: Purification
Mantra: "Om Bara Manidani Svaha"
Action: recite Ksitigarbha's mantra for extinguishing fixed karma 3 times to connect to Ksitigarbha's blessing. It's the process of washing away turbid energy and old karmic obstruction and preparing a clean channel within so the energy that follows can flow well.

Step 2: Invocation
Mantra: "Om Yeommantaja Svaha"
Action: recite Ksitigarbha's heart mantra 3 times to call the energy. The stage of directly inviting Ksitigarbha's energy into the clear space purified in the previous step and making it manifest firmly at your inner center.

Step 3: Offering
Mantra: "Om Namyamani Svaha"
Action: recite Ksitigarbha's offering mantra 3 times to complete the connection. A process of relaxation: expressing deep gratitude and reverence to the holy energy enshrined within, letting go of deliberate control, and surrendering so that energy flows comfortably along the whole body's neural network.
