## Kriya Yoga heart cross with the Vasudeva mantra
[Original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2090026443661275366) (quotes [the Aug 19 post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2089971750968938724))

Cross-cutting the karmic obstacles of the heart with the Kriya Yoga mantra (Heart Cross Variation / The Prayer of the Heart)

Using the 12-syllable Vasudeva mantra (Om Namo Bhagavate Vasudevaya), strike the heart chakra crosswise: left–right, up–down, front–back.

1. Horizontal cross (left–right traverse): fusion of ida and pingala

Moving right: starting on the left of the heart chakra on the back (about 6 cm out), move the energy horizontally to the symmetrical point on the right. Meanwhile vibrate inwardly "Om (left) – Na (center of the chest, anahata) – Mo (right)."

Returning left: bring the energy back from the right point to the left starting point,

vibrating "Bha (right) – Ga (center of the chest, anahata) – Ba (left)."

2. Vertical cross (up–down traverse): fusion of prana and apana

Moving up (subtle in-breath): feeling the energy rise slightly from below to above near the spine on the back, vibrate "Te – Va – Su."

Moving down (subtle out-breath): feeling the energy come back down from above, vibrate "De – Va – Ya" long.

These four energy movements (left–right and back, up–down and back) together complete one cross cycle across the heart.

Do this 108 times.

## Talabya Kriya: stretching the frenulum instead of cutting it
[Original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2089927155547570558) (quotes [the Aug 19 post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2089857345434407138))

The upper wall of the nasopharynx at the back of the throat is dense with vagus nerve endings and endocrine-regulating reflex points. Stimulating this area brings a state of deep relaxation and mental bliss.

For this, yoga traditionally does "Kechari Mudra" (the technique of turning the tongue back and up into the nasopharynx behind the throat).

In classical hatha yoga of the past, a surgical method of cutting the frenulum (the cord under the tongue) little by little with a knife was sometimes used,

but orthodox Kriya Yoga in the Lahiri Mahasaya tradition, instead of such dangerous methods, approaches it through Talabya Kriya, gradually lengthening the frenulum and strengthening the tongue muscles.

[Method]

1. Seal to the palate: stick not just the tip but the whole surface of the tongue broadly to the roof of the mouth (from the hard palate to the soft palate at the back). Draw out the air in the mouth to create a vacuum.

2. Open the jaw (stretching): keeping the tongue vacuum-stuck to the palate, slowly lower the jaw as far as possible. You'll feel a strong stimulus as the frenulum under the tongue is pulled taut.

3. Make the click (Release): when the jaw is fully open and the pressure reaches its limit, release the tongue from the palate so that a loud, clear suction pop (clicking sound), "ttak!" or "peok!", sounds in the mouth.

4. Repeat: counting this as one, repeat rhythmically 50 times.

— Ennio Nimis
