## Following gladly, not dragging by force: Sui ䷐
[Original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2115673907097375154) (quotes [the Oct 20 post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2112365901437919440))

Following gladly, not dragging by force.
*Lake-Thunder, Following* (택뢰수 澤雷隨 ䷐: outer trigram Dui ☱ + inner trigram Zhen ☳)

*Following* (䷐) is the state in which, once the true qi within (Zhen ☳) first moves on the inside, my awareness and breath (Dui ☱) simply follow behind it softly and gladly (수 隨).

It is the hexagram that most exactly expresses "the mind follows the qi" (심수기 心隨氣: the mind quietly follows the flow of qi) and "neither forget nor help" (물망물조 勿忘勿助: do not forget it, and do not force it along).

"Metal and wood uniting and fire hidden in water," beyond metal overcoming wood (금극목 金剋木), that is, metal and wood joining (금목상합 金木相合) and fire stored in water (수중장화 水中藏火):

The outer trigram Dui (☱) is metal (金, the youngest daughter), and the inner trigram Zhen (☳) is wood (木, the eldest son).

In internal alchemy (내단학 內丹學), joining the Azure Dragon of the east (wood 木 · nature 性 · Zhen ☳) with the White Tiger of the west (metal 金 · feeling 情 · Dui ☱) into one is called "metal and wood merging" (금목교병 金木交倂) or "the dragon and tiger mating" (용호상교 龍虎相交).

The sharp metal (Dui ☱) becomes a soft pond of water and embraces the thunder-wood (Zhen ☳) below. So the yang qi does not explode upward and scatter. Instead its warm vibration of life melts wholly into the cool pond (elixir saliva, jade fluid 단침·옥액), and the finest kind of storing and warm nurturing (온양 溫養) takes place.

"Achievement through going with the grain (순리 順理), and the blessing of people (인복 人福)": even if I don't strain to beat the world by force, by reading the flow of the times and the hearts of others and following flexibly (수 隨), I instead draw people and the world to follow me gladly (천하수지 天下隨之). It brings a powerful affinity and a fortune with a fair wind behind it.

The two-hand seals (수결 手訣) and the asymmetric harmony of the fingertip terminals:

Left hand (Zhen ☳): press only the ring finger (output · Triple Burner) flat against the thumb, one to one, and extend the index and middle fingers.

Right hand (Dui ☱): touch the middle finger (control) and ring finger (output) to the thumb and lift only the index finger (input).

How the terminals work: with the ring finger (output) locked on both hands to stop qi from leaking, only the index-finger (input) antenna of the right hand is opened wide. It gladly receives and follows (right hand Dui ☱) the vibration rising from within (left hand Zhen ☳).

## Tea, the gut, and the Industrial Revolution
[Original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2115623722086436933) (quotes [a post by another account](https://x.com/i/status/2115378388047102404))

The principle of qi that operates in an individual body operates the same way in the history of civilization.

Medieval Europeans drank alcohol (fermented drinks) all day instead of contaminated water, and the whole society was sunk in alcohol-made "damp-phlegm" (습담, turbid qi) and brain fog.

But in the 17th century, as "tea," steeped in boiled water, spread, a huge shift occurred.

According to research by historians such as Alan Macfarlane, the biggest secret behind the population explosion in early Industrial Revolution Britain, even as workers crowded into unsanitary, packed cities, was that the habit of drinking black tea sharply cut deaths from intestinal disease.

With healthy intestines (장 腸), labor productivity and vitality leapt.

The sterilizing power of boiled water and tea washed out gut toxins and waterborne disease, and tea's clear, alert energy cleared away the heavy meat diet and intoxication of Europeans. Once the turbid qi in the belly went down and the collective head cleared, the Enlightenment and the Industrial Revolution finally began.

What we drink, and how we tune the qi of the body, changes a far larger reality than we think.

## Food, turbid qi, and why aged Pu-erh clears the head
[Original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2115604968447320119) (quotes [the Oct 17 post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2111253190553559456))

The qi of our body doesn't end inside the body. It resonates constantly with outside qi (외기 外氣) and produces effects in reality.

This is so-called "resonance of like qi" (동기감응 同氣感應).

So clearly tuning the qi within me is the heart of daily life, and what affects the body's qi most directly is "food." Unfortunately, modern food culture developed while leaving out the concept of qi, so there are far too many foods that may taste sweet and delicious on the tongue but make the inner qi turbid. The typical example is fatty meat.

When heavy fat sits in the digestive tract, the body concentrates blood and energy on the stomach to process it. Blood flow to the brain drops, and brain fog sets in, the head dull as if covered in mist.

That's because grease and toxins stagnating in the gut keep sending "discomfort and stress signals" up the vagus nerve to the brain.

Eastern medicine calls this "damp-phlegm" (습담 濕痰: sticky waste and turbid qi).

When greasy food like pork belly blocks the middle burner (중초, the digestive system), the turbid qi that should go down instead surges upward and weighs heavily on the head.

At such times, the deep aged aroma and medicinal aroma of well-aged old tea (Pu-erh) open up the blocked qi refreshingly (통기 通氣) and sweep the heat and damp-phlegm of the liver and gallbladder strongly downward.

Emptying the turbid qi downward is what finally lets the upper body (the head) become clear. The physiology of the body and the principle of qi turn together, meshed like this.
