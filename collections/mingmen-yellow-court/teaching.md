## 1. [Korean Meditation] A practice that goes beyond meditation to change the structure of the body
*Nov 18, 2026 · [original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2122855871235674340)* · [audio](audio/01-2122855871235674340.mp3)

Many meditation methods stop at watching thoughts or the breath. But Korea's Guseon (구선스님) unravels practice not as a concept but as a thorough "engineering of bioenergy and the purification of the skeleton."

This is because of his insight that emotions and karmic imprints (업식 業識), suppressed for a long time, are physically engraved beyond the organs (장기 臟器) into the bones, the spine and even the tailbone. Follow the core roadmap of "The method of contemplation (관 觀) using qi (기 氣)," printed in the appendix of his book, and a clear map is drawn of how the unmoving mind and the Yellow Court (황정 黃庭), the inner brazier, are established.

1. The great principle of practice: the organs (장부 臟腑) must be cleared first before the bones open

The five-stage flow of the whole practice that Guseon presents is as follows: Om (OM) practice → purification of the organs → formation of the center → deliverance of karmic imprints (purification of the bones) → progress of the center.

There is an order here that must be kept. The turbid energy of the organs (yin qi) is discharged outward through the meridians; if you force practices that open the bones while the organs are not yet purified, the yin qi of the organs flows back into the bones and causes terrible pain. So the first button is to loosen the five organs and six bowels with the vibration of a low "Om" and to establish a still "center" in the chest ground (가슴바탕; 1 cm above the solar plexus and 5 cm into the chest), free of any wavering of emotion.

2. Opening the five hearts (오심 五心) and purifying the spine: making the body one "empty tube"

When the chest ground has become still and you can watch the outer world and your inner center at the same time (separation of consciousness), the purification of the bones finally begins. The order is vertebrae → ribs → tailbone → skull.

The first gates to open in order to bore through the spine are the five hearts (오심 五心): the two palms (Laogong, 노궁), the two soles (Yongquan, 용천) and the crown (Baihui, 백회).

The palms (Laogong) and Mingmen: feel the opening in the middle of the palm through the vibration of the fingertips and palm, gather the energy like a ping-pong ball, and on the in-breath draw it along the arm bones, shoulders and Dazhui to Mingmen at the back (L2–L3). (Here the thumb is linked directly to the vibration of the lungs, and the index finger to that of the large intestine.)

The soles (Yongquan) and Mingmen: lying down with the body relaxed, push the hot heat of Mingmen down to the soles on the out-breath, and when a pulsing vibration forms in the soles, draw it back up to Mingmen on the in-breath.

Baihui and Mingmen: on the in-breath, draw the vibration of the crown straight down the exact middle of the spine to Mingmen.

Once all five gates are open, you no longer put your will on individual points. Treating the whole spine as one hollow tube, with each in-breath you let the energy of the whole body be drawn in through the spine to Mingmen and hang there like a ball.

3. Rib purification and the birth of heart-fire (심화 心火): tearing down the iron wall of the mind

When energy has condensed in Mingmen like a ball and the stillness of the chest ground is stable enough to overpower that energy, the Former Heaven qi of Mingmen flows through Lingtai at the back (around T5) into the center of the chest.

At this point the chest fills with energy and you feel a tautness as if a solid "iron wall" (철벽 鐵壁; a wall to face, 면벽 面壁) had been raised. Whatever outer situation comes, you simply keep watching this wall in the chest in silence and wait for it to crack.

Now add the breath.

In-breath: with intention, divide the energy of the chest into the ribs on both sides and send it to Lingtai at the back. With a ringing thrill, the area around Lingtai tightens.

Out-breath: from Lingtai as the base, voice "Om" toward the middle dantian and draw the energy of Lingtai into the middle of the chest.

As you repeat this, at some moment the solid iron wall bursts open and the chest ground is emptied like open sky. And in that empty place a soft, warm energy, heart-fire (심화 心火), blooms and seeps down into the lower belly like a spring breeze. This is the moment the five stages of the progress of the chest ground (stillness → penetrating equanimity → like an iron wall → bursting open → the birth of heart-fire) are complete.

4. Purifying the tailbone and building the Yellow Court (황정 黃庭): the furnace that melts all afflictions

Guseon sees the tailbone as the place where the primal survival instinct, the will to fight and sexual desire left in human beings are stored, along with the karmic imprints that overflow from the spine. The final energy center built to purify the turbid qi of this tailbone is exactly the Yellow Court.

The key to building the Yellow Court lies in the "lower-dantian Om" and the "union of heart-fire and Former Heaven qi."

The position of the tongue tip: lightly touch the tip of the tongue to the inside of the lower gums and voice "Om" in a low tone so that the lower belly rings, making the lower dantian and Mingmen at the back resonate.

The crossing of in-breath and out-breath: when hot Former Heaven qi hangs in Mingmen, draw that heat to the lower dantian on the in-breath and heat it like a ball of fire, and on the out-breath send it back to Mingmen with the lower-dantian "Om."

The place where it "catches" with a click: as you repeat sending the warm heart-fire that came down from the chest to Mingmen, a point appears where the kidneys pull the energy and it catches with a "tak—."

This is exactly the Yellow Court, the furnace of life force in which four energies fuse into one: Later Heaven qi (the breath) + Former Heaven qi (Mingmen) + heart-fire (the chest) + kidney fire (the kidneys).

5. Life after the Yellow Court: a brazier on which snowflakes melt

Once the Yellow Court is established, the landscape of practice changes completely. Even when all kinds of emotional states rise, anger, jealousy, affection, fear, they are no more than a single snowflake falling in front of a blazing brazier (the Yellow Court). They melt without a trace before they even touch it.

Guseon says that at this stage there is no need even to work hard at "Om" practice separately; you only need to make it resonate with the ringing of the lower dantian about once every thirty minutes, and, even while walking through daily life, gaze gently at the Yellow Court alone as if holding a baby.

In the end, practice is not an austerity of suppressing thought. It is the work of boring through the blocked paths of the five hearts and the spine, the ribs and the tailbone, and lighting one warm brazier that never goes out in the very middle of your body.

## 6. Kidney fire: the fire within water
*Nov 3, 2026 · [original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2117581896452571384)* · [audio](audio/06-2117581896452571384.mp3)

In Eastern internal alchemy, the Former Heaven fire that blooms deep inside the kidneys, the organ that governs water, is called "kidney fire" (신화 腎火) or "the fire within water" (수중지화 水中之火).

At first glance this idea of "fire inside water" sounds like a contradiction. But looked at through the lens of modern renal physiology and neuroendocrinology, it is an insight that precisely captures the combination of three things: the mitochondrial metabolism of the kidneys, which produce more electricity and heat per unit of weight than anything else in the body; the bioelectricity of the nerve plexus behind the peritoneum; and the endocrine hormone axis that governs the bone marrow and the vitality of the whole body.

The kidneys are not a passive strainer that merely filters urine. After the heart (and higher even than the brain), they are the body's "ultra-high-output power plant," with overwhelmingly high mitochondrial density and oxygen consumption per unit of weight.

The two kidneys filter about 180 liters of plasma (water) a day and then reabsorb 99% of that water and electrolytes (sodium, potassium, calcium and so on) back into the blood. In this process, the ultra-dense mitochondria inside the tubule cells synthesize and burn enormous amounts of ATP. By the laws of thermodynamics, a considerable amount of energy is released as deep metabolic heat (thermal energy) as ATP is made and broken down.

Under chronic stress or sympathetic tension, the renal blood vessels constrict, and the inside of the kidneys (especially the medulla) is left in a chronic low-oxygen state.

But when, in stillness, you gently regulate the pressure of the abdominal cavity with slack in the lower belly and ultra-fine breathing, renal blood flow opens and the dense mitochondria of the tubules are all supplied with oxygen and switch on at once. Then, in the middle of kidney tissue through which 180 liters of fluid (water) circulate, explosive ATP bioelectricity and deep metabolic heat (fire) bloom up. This is the felt reality of the "fire within water" (kidney fire) that a practitioner senses deep inside the lower back.

Step 2.

First method: the double intake of the in-breath through Laogong (the bones) and the skin of the whole body (charging the Mingmen battery)

This is the process in which "on the in-breath, breath comes in through Laogong and the bones and gathers in Mingmen." It is the core stage in which Later Heaven qi is used as kindling to set fire to the Former Heaven original qi (kundalini) of Mingmen.

In-breath (Laogong → arm bones → vertebrae → Mingmen, together with whole-body skin breathing):

1) Laogong-into-the-bone intake: As you breathe in, suck the energy ball in through the opening of Laogong in the palm as if snatching it, and draw it down in one go through the wrist bones → forearm bones → shoulder blades → Dazhui (대추혈 大椎穴, T1), then along the core of the vertebrae to Mingmen (L2–L3).

2) Skin-to-spine intake: At the same time as you draw energy in through Laogong, let the skin of the whole body draw the energy of the outer space (Later Heaven qi) into the center of the spine, as if tightening toward the spinal column, and join it at Mingmen with the energy from Laogong.

Out-breath (settling at Mingmen with the sound "Om," or returning to Laogong):

Early stage (while purifying the spine): On the out-breath, send the energy back from Mingmen through the spine → Dazhui → arm bones → both palms (Laogong), opening the channel inside the bones (microtubules, periosteum) back and forth.

Advanced stage (when charging Mingmen intensively): On the out-breath, voice "Om—" so that it resonates in the center of the chest, and watch as the energy gathered on the in-breath resonates strongly at Mingmen (and at the four points: lower dantian, Lingtai and the center) and is amplified into a hot mass the size of a basketball. Step 3.

Second method: back-and-forth breathing between the lower dantian and Mingmen, and activating the kidneys (forming the Yellow Court). Once Mingmen has caught fire, this is the process in which energy is moved back and forth through the slanted three-cun tube (cave) between the lower dantian at the front and Mingmen at the back, fully waking the kidneys (신장 腎臟) on either side.

It proceeds as follows, depending on your stage of practice and how you use the heels and abdominal pressure.

Led by awareness and a physical switch (the trajectory "in-breath: lower dantian > Mingmen / out-breath: Mingmen → lower dantian")

In-breath (condensing from the lower dantian → Mingmen): As you breathe in, push the energy and abdominal pressure that came into the lower belly (lower dantian) back toward the spine (using the pull of the crura of the diaphragm on L2–L3, linked with a switch of pressing the heels gently into the floor), compressing and concentrating the energy deep from the lower dantian into Mingmen. At this point the left and right kidneys and adrenal glands on either side of Mingmen are strongly pressed and stimulated.

Out-breath (spreading from Mingmen → lower dantian): As you breathe out (with the sound "Om" or a soundless micro-breath, plus a switch of giving the heels a little slack), let the Former Heaven original qi that was tightly compressed in Mingmen push out warmly through the three-cun channel from Mingmen to the lower dantian at the front, soaking the whole abdominal cavity (the enteric nervous system).

Step 4.

The final fruit: purification of kundalini and its union with the kidneys' Former Heaven qi (kidney fire) → the birth of the Yellow Court (黃庭)

These are the physiological and energetic changes in the kidneys that occur as the energy travels back and forth between the lower dantian and Mingmen.

Purifying the coarse heat: The kundalini that first wakes in Mingmen (high sympathetic heat) is rough and hot, but as it moves back and forth between the lower dantian (acetylcholine, parasympathetic energy) and Mingmen while connected to the stillness of the center of the chest (no-mind Zen, no-thought Zen), its natural frequency calms, and it is refined into soft, gentle energy with no sense of anything foreign.

Extracting the kidneys' Former Heaven qi (the energy of the hun soul, the fire within water): The moment the kundalini of Mingmen settles quietly and softly, the "Former Heaven qi of the kidneys" (kidney fire, 신화 腎火) contained in the left and right kidneys on either side of L2–L3 (the clusters of dense mitochondria in the tubules) is stimulated as if drawn by a magnet and comes out to the middle.

Gathering of the Yellow Court (the 11th stage): At the exact midpoint of the line connecting the lower dantian and Mingmen (in front of L2–L3), "the purified kundalini of Mingmen + the One Thing of the lower dantian + the Former Heaven qi of the left and right kidneys" join into one, and the blazing brazier-fire of the Yellow Court is formed.

1. Preparation: touch thumb and index finger and feel the pulse in the palms → after draining the cold from the fingers, make a warm ball in the palms.

2. In-breath (charging): breathe in simultaneously through Laogong in the palms (the arm bone to vertebra route) and the skin of the whole body, compressing the energy deep from the lower dantian into Mingmen at the back (L2–L3).

3. Out-breath (circulating): with the sound "Om—" (or a fine out-breath), watch the warm energy of Mingmen and both kidneys push out through the tube to the lower dantian (keep the center of the chest down to the lower dantian quiet, as an "empty jar").

4. Condensing: when the back-and-forth movement becomes smooth, contemplate the energy of both kidneys gathering at the midpoint between the lower dantian and Mingmen (the Yellow Court) and a hot brazier-fire forming there.

## 12. Why "relax" doesn't work in breathing and meditation
*Nov 23, 2026 · [original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2124682926491336710)* · [audio](audio/12-2124682926491336710.mp3)

Why the words "relax" don't work well when you breathe or meditate.

Just releasing the force of the outer muscles doesn't fully release the diaphragm and the solar plexus. The diaphragm and the solar plexus (the celiac plexus) are not simple muscles; they are where the survival instinct and the sympathetic defense mechanisms bolt the door most firmly. The more you intend with your head, "let's get comfortable," the more fine tension piles up.

So rather than straining to release force from the outside, you must first fill energy and circulation from the inside (the lower dantian, Mingmen, the Yellow Court).

Physiologically too, the diaphragm releases not in response to the cerebrum's command "relax" but to a "complete safety signal" rising from deep in the abdominal cavity. When the warm circulation and density inside have filled enough to warm the ribcage (Zhongji) from within, only then does the diaphragm, which had been lifted and stiff, drop with a thud, and even the intention to breathe disappears.

That is why a feeling of expanding comfortably like a balloon comes, even without forcing the chest open.

In the end, real relaxation is not a technique of releasing force from outside; it is the result of fullness filling up inside and pushing out the outer tension by itself.

If you try to get rid of thoughts or force yourself to contemplate when you meditate, you soon get exhausted. In the end you are trying to catch thoughts with thoughts, so only the frontal lobe tenses.

The biggest advantage of energy practice (the bottom-up approach) is that once you learn the flow of energy, you enter absorption (선정 禪定) by itself.

If you keep the body's energy circuits open, the lower dantian, Mingmen and the Yellow Court, the autonomic nerves descend into a deep parasympathetic stability and the power of the brain's distraction circuit (the DMN) switches off on its own. It is not pressing the waves down by hand; it is putting the wind itself to sleep.

Only, as the energy grows stronger, don't lose the "unbothered ground" (아무렇지 않은 바탕) that watches the flow calmly, so that you don't get drunk on the energy sensations or carried away.

In the end, the flow of energy is the fastest highway that goes straight to absorption without wrestling with distracting thoughts.
