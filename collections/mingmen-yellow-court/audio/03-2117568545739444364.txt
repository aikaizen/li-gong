[calm, unhurried, contemplative teacher reading a meditation text] Why a cup of Pu'er before and after dantian breathing helps. A post from November 3. [break] The cold in the abdomen that comes from over-activation of the parasympathetic nerves arises when acetylcholine stagnates at the nerve endings.

Well-aged old tea or genuine Pu'er has an outstanding synergy, both neurobiochemically and in terms of practice, for releasing the yin-fire (cold stagnation) caused by narrowing of the vagal nerve endings in the lower dantian and excess build-up of acetylcholine.

The key macromolecules produced as Pu'er post-ferments over the years, theabrownins, gallic acid and oxidized catechins, stimulate the gut microbiota and promote nitric oxide (NO) secretion from the endothelial cells of the intestinal lining.

Nitric oxide immediately relaxes stiffened intestinal smooth muscle and micro-vessels, widening the space of the vagal nerve-ending mesh that had physically narrowed.

At the same time, as warm blood circulates, the acidification of the tissue (lowered pH) is relieved, creating the best conditions for the enzyme that breaks down acetylcholine (AChE), which had been stuck in substrate inhibition, to break down and reset acetylcholine again.

In addition, L-theanine, an amino acid abundant in Pu'er, crosses the blood-brain barrier (BBB), raises levels of the inhibitory neurotransmitter GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid) in the brain, and induces alpha and theta waves.

This gently calms the runaway upper medulla (the dorsal motor nucleus of the vagus), which had been pouring out acetylcholine, over-excited by stress or forced breathing.
