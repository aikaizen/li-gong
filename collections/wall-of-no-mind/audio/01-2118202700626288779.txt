[calm, unhurried, contemplative teacher reading a meditation text] The character for qi with fire under nothing: fire inside nothingness. A post from November 5. [break] The Daoist character for qi that is written with fire under nothing is not the ordinary character for the qi of breathing. It is a character used specifically in Daoism to mean the Former Heaven original qi (the true one qi of Former Heaven) that awakens in the state of no-thought, when thinking has been cut off.

The very shape of the character, "fire" placed beneath "nothing", perfectly depicts the state of holding the flame of Ding fire inside that empty nothingness.

While we write the Later Heaven energy we get by eating rice and breathing air through the nose with the ordinary character, we distinguish the Former Heaven energy that ignites by itself in absolute stillness, where thought has been completely cut off, by writing it with the character of fire under nothing.

The site of Zhonghuang (the thalamus and third ventricle), midway between Mixin (between the eyebrows) and Yuzhen (the back of the head), is the "absolutely empty space" (the place of no-thought) where even the sensation of breathing is cut off completely and all deliberation and discrimination (thought) vanish.

If thought and intention remain (having-thought), only the coarse Later Heaven fire-qi of the sympathetic nerves blazes (ordinary fire, not samadhi fire), and the Former Heaven energy never awakens.

In other words, the upper "nothing" signifies the zero-point state in which the thalamus and hypothalamus, the brain's center for integrating the senses, are completely emptied.

If Bing fire is the sun-fire blazing fiercely outward (sympathetic overdrive, anger, lust), Ding fire is the candle, the moonlight, the seed-fire in the brazier: cool and wrapped in quiet darkness (yin) on the outside, yet shining unextinguished at its core.

In Guseon's system, after passing through the kidney fire of the Yellow Court (the fire within water) and being absorbed into the bones (the marrow), the "One Thing" that settles densely in the empty place of no-thought of Zhonghuang in the head, and the "clear, transparent form of quiescent extinction" (the vitality of being alive) that hangs just beneath it (the hypothalamus), are exactly this "Ding fire inside nothingness."
