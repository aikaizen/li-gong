[calm, unhurried, contemplative teacher reading a meditation text] Composure is a trained neural boundary (English). A post from November 26. [break] Most people think composure means using willpower to force your muscles not to flinch.

[break] Traditional Korean somatic training teaches the opposite: separating awareness from the surface of the body.

When a threat is an inch from your temple, your brainstem screams flinch. But when inhibitory gating in the thalamus builds what Zen masters call the “Wall of No-Mind”, the stimulus hits the surface—and stops there.

[break] The gap between chaos and panic isn’t willpower. It’s a trained neural boundary.
