[calm, unhurried, contemplative teacher reading a meditation text] The Wall of No-Mind, the Thalamus and the Bright Nature. [break] The Daoist character, fire under 'nothing', names the Former Heaven qi that ignites only when thought is cut off, at Zhonghuang, the empty center of the head around the thalamus. He locates the wall of no-mind just inside the line joining the temples and the root of the nose, at the GABA gate of the thalamic reticular nucleus. Raising it cuts the senses and the frontal lobe's evaluating loop, the brake on the basal forebrain lifts, and a joy with no object wells up: the bright nature. He reads it through Lacan, Spinoza and Damasio, through the Longmen chart whose three inner channels converge on the midbrain, and, in his English posts, through Guseon's Spiral Breathwork, a downward spiral of inhibition from the crown to the thalamus, and through the goat on the cliff: no-thought is not a blank mind but a nervous system with zero latency.
