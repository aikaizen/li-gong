[calm, unhurried, contemplative teacher reading a meditation text] Lacan's surplus enjoyment and the wall of no-mind. A post from November 13. [break] As Lacan said, the reason people cannot let go of the repetition compulsion of painful thoughts and feelings (karmic imprints) is the thin surplus enjoyment (plus-de-jouir) hidden inside the symptom. Willpower alone can never cut that sweet attachment.

If you raise a wall of no-mind at the place where the temples and the root of the nose meet, and the joy and flutter of the bright nature fill up inside it, the intentional tendencies that were originally placed in consciousness, emotion and will are cut off by themselves, and you abide in joy on your own.

An overwhelming, self-sufficient immanent enjoyment (the dharma-bliss of the bright nature), beyond compare with the shabby scraps of pleasure squeezed out of outer objects or delusions, bursts out of the basal forebrain and the chest, so the brain completely loses any reason to cling any longer to outer representations or the dregs of affliction.

It shows itself as what the late Lacan likened to the mystics' ecstasy, "the jouissance of the Other" (feminine, non-verbal enjoyment): the rapture of pure life itself, filling up hazily and softly.
