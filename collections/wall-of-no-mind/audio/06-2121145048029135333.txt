[calm, unhurried, contemplative teacher reading a meditation text] Spinoza's joy and the bright nature. A post from November 13. [break] For Spinoza, the affect that blooms by itself within when the power of being expresses itself without obstruction is "joy" (Laetitia).

[break] That immanent joy, with no outer cause,
is what the bright nature produces:.

[break] when the wall of no-mind is raised between the thalamus and the brow,
and, gazing at that wall of no-mind, you stop the stimuli coming from outside,.

[break] then the physiological phenomenon called joy arises. This is called the rapture of meditative absorption.

[break] Spinoza called it sadness when this bright feeling is diminished by the discrimination of good and bad.

He called the state in which the conatus is distorted, swept about by encounters (affections) with outer objects, "bondage" (passive passions), and in Buddhism these are called greed, hatred and delusion.
