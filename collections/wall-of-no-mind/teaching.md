## 1. The character 炁: fire inside nothingness
*Nov 5, 2026 · [original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2118202700626288779)* · [audio](audio/01-2118202700626288779.mp3)

The character 炁 (qi; 既 → 無 + 灬) is not the ordinary 氣 (qi) of breathing. It is a character used specifically in Daoism to mean the Former Heaven original qi (선천의 원기; 先天眞一之炁, the true one qi of Former Heaven) that awakens in the state of no-thought (무념 無念), when thinking has been cut off.

The very shape of the character, "fire" (灬) placed beneath "nothing" (無), perfectly depicts the state of holding the flame of Ding fire (정화 丁火) inside that empty nothingness.

While we write the Later Heaven energy we get by eating rice (米) and breathing air (气) through the nose as 氣, we distinguish the Former Heaven energy that ignites by itself in absolute stillness, where thought has been completely cut off, by writing it 炁.

The site of Zhonghuang (중황 中黃; the thalamus and third ventricle), midway between Mixin (미심 眉心, between the eyebrows) and Yuzhen (옥침 玉枕, the back of the head), is the "absolutely empty space" (the place of no-thought, 無念處) where even the sensation of breathing is cut off completely and all deliberation and discrimination (thought) vanish.

If thought and intention remain (having-thought, 유념 有念), only the coarse Later Heaven fire-qi of the sympathetic nerves blazes (ordinary fire, 범화 凡火, not samadhi fire), and the Former Heaven energy never awakens.

In other words, the upper "無" signifies the zero-point state in which the thalamus and hypothalamus, the brain's center for integrating the senses, are completely emptied.

If Bing fire (병화 丙火) is the sun-fire blazing fiercely outward (sympathetic overdrive, anger, lust), Ding fire is the candle, the moonlight, the seed-fire in the brazier: cool and wrapped in quiet darkness (yin, 無) on the outside, yet shining unextinguished at its core.

In Guseon's (구선스님) system, after passing through the kidney fire of the Yellow Court (the fire within water) and being absorbed into the bones (the marrow), the "One Thing" (한 물건) that settles densely in the empty place of no-thought of Zhonghuang in the head, and the "clear, transparent form of quiescent extinction" (적멸상 寂滅相; the vitality of being alive) that hangs just beneath it (the hypothalamus), are exactly this "Ding fire inside nothingness."

## 3. The wall of no-mind and the joy of the bright nature
*Nov 13, 2026 · [original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2121053626718372162)* · [audio](audio/03-2121053626718372162.mp3)

When you enter meditative absorption (선정 禪定), if you raise a wall of no-mind (무심의 벽) at a particular place, then without any outside stimulus a "dreamy, intoxicated joy and a flutter in the chest" wells up by itself.

This is called the awakening of the bright nature (밝은 성품).

That place is just inside the horizontal line that joins the temples (Taiyang point, 태양혈 太陽穴) and Shangen (산근 山根), where the nose begins.

When you raise the "wall of no-mind" here, the joy and flutter of the bright nature arise..

This is exactly the vertical cross-section running from the basal forebrain, the pleasure center (the septal nuclei and nucleus accumbens), to the hypothalamus and pituitary.

In ordinary waking consciousness, the prefrontal cortex (thinking, planning, worry) constantly presses down on the nucleus accumbens and septal nuclei at the floor of the limbic system through glutamate-GABA inhibitory circuits. It is a structure that lifts this brake for a moment to give a reward only "when you achieve or gain something."

But if you raise a "wall of no-mind" between the brow and the thalamus and cut off at once the inflow of the senses through eyes, ears, nose and mouth and the discriminating loop of the frontal lobe, the frontal lobe's conditional inhibitory signal that was pressing on the nucleus accumbens and septal nuclei disappears (disinhibition).

Anatomically, the space between Mixin (the prefrontal cortex) and the thalamus is the "anterior thalamic radiation," the passage along which sensory information and thought loops shuttle ceaselessly, and the stretch of the brain that burns the most glucose and oxygen. When you raise the wall of no-mind here and cut this circuit, reactions driven by outside stimuli are cut off (from the practice point of view, of course).

In particular, the baseline dopamine signal rising from the midbrain (the ventral tegmental area, VTA, the headquarters of dopamine production) strikes the walls of the nucleus accumbens and septal nuclei and pools there intact, with nothing spent outward, and a pure inner elation (flutter) with no conditions at all switches on spontaneously.

Modern affective neuroscience (the research of Kent Berridge and others) strictly distinguishes pleasure into dopaminergic "wanting," which chases objects outward, and "pure liking" (bliss), based on opioids, endorphins and anandamide, which melts fully into this very place, now.

Because the wall of no-mind closes four of the faculties (eye, ear, nose, tongue) and cuts off at the source the channel of "wanting" aimed at outer objects, only the tiny "hedonic hotspots" in the inner shell of the nucleus accumbens and the ventral pallidum resonate on their own.

In the end, if the everyday joy of ordinary beings goes through the costly detour of "outer sensory stimulus → positive evaluation by the frontal lobe → temporary release of the brake,"

the joy created by the wall of no-mind is the phenomenon in which, by switching off the sensory floodgates and the frontal lobe's evaluating device altogether, the baseline bliss of life itself (the bright nature) that the basal forebrain (septal nuclei, nucleus accumbens, hypothalamus) originally held is revealed without obstruction.

## 4. Where the wall of no-mind stands: the GABA gate of the thalamus
*Nov 14, 2026 · [original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2121374067366490564)* · [audio](audio/04-2121374067366490564.mp3)

The place where the wall of no-mind is raised is the location of the thalamic reticular nucleus, which releases GABA.

TRN: made up almost entirely of neurons that secrete only the inhibitory neurotransmitter GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid), nearly the only such structure in the whole thalamus.

When the GABA-TRN is activated, it inhibits the thalamic relay nuclei and blocks unnecessary sensory signals (surrounding noise, trivial touch and so on) from reaching the cerebral cortex.

## 5. Lacan's surplus enjoyment and the wall of no-mind
*Nov 13, 2026 · [original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2121054893540806839)* · [audio](audio/05-2121054893540806839.mp3)

As Lacan said, the reason people cannot let go of the repetition compulsion of painful thoughts and feelings (karmic imprints, 업식 業識) is the thin surplus enjoyment (plus-de-jouir) hidden inside the symptom. Willpower alone can never cut that sweet attachment.

If you raise a wall of no-mind (무심벽) at the place where the temples and the root of the nose meet, and the joy and flutter of the bright nature fill up inside it, the intentional tendencies (유위 有爲) that were originally placed in consciousness, emotion and will are cut off by themselves, and you abide in joy on your own.

An overwhelming, self-sufficient immanent enjoyment (the dharma-bliss of the bright nature), beyond compare with the shabby scraps of pleasure squeezed out of outer objects or delusions, bursts out of the basal forebrain and the chest, so the brain completely loses any reason to cling any longer to outer representations or the dregs of affliction.

It shows itself as what the late Lacan likened to the mystics' ecstasy, "the jouissance of the Other" (feminine, non-verbal enjoyment): the rapture of pure life itself, filling up hazily and softly.

## 6. Spinoza's joy and the bright nature
*Nov 13, 2026 · [original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2121145048029135333)* · [audio](audio/06-2121145048029135333.mp3)

For Spinoza, the affect that blooms by itself within when the power of being expresses itself without obstruction is "joy" (Laetitia).

That immanent joy, with no outer cause,
is what the bright nature produces:

when the wall of no-mind is raised between the thalamus and the brow,
and, gazing at that wall of no-mind, you stop the stimuli coming from outside,

then the physiological phenomenon called joy arises.
This is called the rapture of meditative absorption (선정의 환희).

Spinoza called it sadness when this bright feeling is diminished by the discrimination of good and bad.

He called the state in which the conatus is distorted, swept about by encounters (affections) with outer objects, "bondage" (passive passions), and in Buddhism these are called greed, hatred and delusion (탐·진·치 貪·瞋·癡).

## 8. Stop Fighting Your Thoughts: The Korean “Spiral Breathwork” Protocol for Thalamic Calm
*Nov 24, 2026 · [original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2125098723206459861)* · [audio](audio/08-2125098723206459861.mp3)

Most Western meditation advice makes the same mistake: it asks a noisy prefrontal cortex to wrestle itself into silence.

You can’t out-think an overactive nervous system.

In a little-known Korean contemplative somatic tradition (systematized by Zen teacher Guseon), genuine mental stillness isn’t treated as a philosophical concept. It’s treated as a downward wave of neural inhibition moving along the brain-to-spinal-cord axis.

Instead of passively “watching your breath,” this protocol pairs a manual nerve switch, a funnel-shaped cortical focus, and bilateral exhale balancing to quiet the brain’s sensory relay station—the thalamus.

Here is the 4-step protocol you can test in 5 minutes.

**Why the Thalamus Matters**

Every sight, sound, and internal stress signal passes through the thalamus before hitting your conscious awareness. When your thalamocortical loop is running hot, your mind races.

When inhibitory signaling (mediated by GABAergic networks like the thalamic reticular nucleus) turns up, sensory static drops and brainwaves naturally slow toward a calm, dreamy theta state.

Spiral Breathwork is a somatic way to steer attention directly down that cortical-to-thalamic funnel.

**Step 1: Unlock the Crown with the “Thumb Switch”**

Before you change your breath, you need to prime the sensory pathway at the top of the skull (the parietal cortex / crown point).

Sit upright with your spine unslumped.

Flex your thumb firmly toward the base of your pinky finger. This subtle hand posture creates a reflex-like co-activation along the facial/maxillary sensory field and the space between your eyebrows (glabella).

Take slow, simultaneous nasal and belly breaths.

Keep your attention split between the spot between your eyebrows and the very top of your head until you feel a faint tightening, tingling, or gentle vortex sensation at the crown.

That sensation means the top of the circuit is online.

**Step 2: The Spiral Inhale (Cortical-to-Thalamic Funnel)**

Now turn that crown sensation into a slow, downward vector.

Imagine a gentle vacuum right at the crown of your head.

As you inhale slowly through your nose, draw that tightening sensation inward and downward in a smooth spiral toward the dead center of your brain (the thalamus).

Critical rule: Match the speed of the spiral to the exact speed of your inhale. Don’t rush it.

Think of the cerebral cortex narrowing like a funnel into the thalamus. After several slow cycles, you’ll notice a distinct shift: the sharp edge of mental chatter softens into a quiet, slightly hazy stillness. That is the subjective signature of thalamic down-regulation.

**Step 3: Extend the “Pillar of Stillness”**

Once the crown-to-thalamus drop feels effortless, extend the path.

On your next slow spiral inhales, let that quiet, damped sensation sink further down—past the thalamus, into the brainstem (medulla), and gradually down the central line of your upper spine.

You aren’t forcing relaxation. You’re building a vertical, quiet “pillar” through your central nervous system where excess noise simply doesn’t propagate.

**Step 4: The Bilateral Exhale (Cranial Nerve Reset)**

Most breathwork ignores what to do with the exhale. In this protocol, inhale inhibits, exhale activates and balances.

Once your inhale reaches the center of the brainstem/upper chest axis, split your exhale symmetrically to the left and right, directing your awareness outward toward both sides of your face.

Scan for subtle asymmetries—around the eyes, ears, jaw, and cheeks.

Repeat until both sides of your face feel equally warm, loose, and balanced.

**The Core Rule to Remember**

- **Inhale (Spiral Down):** Dampen excess sensory noise along the cortex → thalamus → brainstem axis.

- **Exhale (Split Outward):** Re-activate and balance left-right cranial nerve tone.

Five minutes of this before deep work—or when your brain won’t shut off at 2 AM—beats an hour of trying to force your mind to “just be present.”

Note: This article explores traditional Korean somatic meditation through a contemplative neuroscience lens for stress regulation and focus, not medical treatment. Stop immediately if you feel dizziness or head pressure.
