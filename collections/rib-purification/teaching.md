## 1. A practice the Dragon Spirit is said to have taught: rib purification
*Nov 18, 2026 · [original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2123057544998568036)* · [audio](audio/01-2123057544998568036.mp3)

Usually, when emotion surges, people say, "Breathe in deeply and send it down to the lower belly." But if you force the breath down while the chest (the middle dantian 중단전 中丹田 and the ribs) and the organs (장부 臟腑) have not been purified, side effects arise.

The passion lodged in the chest (heat, sympathetic arousal) is pressed hard from top to bottom without ever being resolved, so instead the belly gets bloated, or the pressure rebounds and shoots up into the head: the rising-qi (상기 上氣) phenomenon.

As a result, the emotional wave that arose in the front of the chest easily gets trapped as it is in the intercostal nerves and rib fascia on both sides, and stiffens into a dull ache.

Rather than sending it down, try breastbone purification.

Instead of stamping emotion down, you open a horizontal rotating circuit that crosses the cylinder of the ribcage from the front (the center, 중심) to the back (Lingtai 영대 靈臺), and refine the energy.

The congestion, the density of passion, lodged in the front of the chest (the center) rides the in-breath as the ribcage widens, travels along the outer edges of the ribs on both sides, and is scattered to Lingtai at the back (around the 5th thoracic vertebra).

Because this spreads emotional energy that was clumped in one spot and about to explode across the broad surface of the left and right ribs, and drains it out through the spinal line at the back, the chest opens up refreshingly at once, without suppression.

On the out-breath, the qi gathered at Lingtai at the back (around T5, where sympathetic ganglia are dense and passion is stored) is drawn back into the center through the straight midline passage, together with the out-breath (and the sounding of "Om").

As this round trip repeats, the emotional turbid qi (탁기 濁氣) wedged in the ribs and the coarse heat of the sympathetic system are washed out, and a firm stillness that outside stimuli cannot shake (the iron wall of no-mind → breaking through → the arising of heart-fire) settles in the center.

When the front-to-back rotation of the ribcage (rib purification) is complete in this way, the ground of the chest is emptied like the void and a warm heart-fire (심화 心火) arises. Then, without any straining to send it down, that soft energy flows down by itself, like the law of gravity, to the lower belly (the lower dantian 하단전 下丹田, the Yellow Court 황정 黃庭).

## 3. Why the chest tightens first: the secret of T5
*Nov 21, 2026 · [original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2124095402823602435)* · [audio](audio/03-2124095402823602435.mp3)

Why does the chest get tight first when you are angry or anxious?

And why, even when you force yourself into belly breathing to calm down, does the knot in the chest not loosen?

The secret is in the middle of the back, at the 5th thoracic vertebra (T5).

It is the very height that the practice of Guseon (구선스님), a Korean esoteric-Buddhist (밀종 密宗) training, calls Lingtai (영대 靈臺) and Zhongji (중극 中極).

Neurophysiologically, T5 is the most important crossroads of the body's autonomic nerves. • Above it, the sympathetic nerves that regulate the heart and lungs end, and • below it, the nerves that govern the stomach, liver, kidneys and the stress hormones (adrenals) are just beginning: it is the boundary line.

What is more, the nerves that branch out from here travel along the muscles between the ribs (intercostals) and wrap around in a circle from the back through the flanks to the front of the chest.

When emotion runs high, this number-5 switch overloads.

The heart pounds, the stomach bloats, and at the same time the muscles between the ribs stiffen like armor. This is the moment emotion is trapped in the body.

That is why "breastbone (rib) purification" (가슴뼈(갈비뼈) 순화) is needed.

We cannot command the autonomic nerves directly: "Calm down right now!" But widening and vibrating the rib muscles is something we can do by will.

The process of spreading the energy of the chest into the ribs on both sides on the in-breath and sending it to the back (Lingtai), then gathering it back to the center on the out-breath with a deep "Om" vibration, is not simple stretching.

It is the work of waking the sensory receptors densely embedded in the rib muscles and sending a physical signal in reverse to the T5 autonomic main switch: "It's safe now; you can turn the tension off."

When this main switch releases, the taut pressure in the chest bursts open, and soft warmth flows down by itself all the way into the belly, without your having to stamp the breath down into the lower belly.

Real, deep relaxation is not pushing out the belly while the chest stays bound. It begins by first throwing open the armor of the ribs, where emotion is trapped.

## 5. Open the ribs before the belly
*Nov 24, 2026 · [original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2125092226925736003)* · [audio](audio/05-2125092226925736003.mp3)

If your belly feels stiff and stuffy when you do abdominal breathing,
there's a good chance you have the order and direction reversed.

1. Open the breastbone (the ribcage) before the belly.

The ribcage is the lid covering the abdominal cavity and the frame from which the diaphragm hangs.

If you try to push breath into the belly first while the upper ribcage is locked, only resistance and tension build in the abdomen. Only when you first open the ribs and empty the lid of the upper body do the lower belly and the depths of the abdominal cavity open softly, without tension.

2. The breastbone must start from the "front," not the "back."

If you try to spread the ribs from the back (thoracic spine) side on the in-breath, they don't open well because of the structure, and only the trapezius, neck and shoulders tense up.

Instead, open softly from the flexible front sternum (around the 5th rib), and breathe in as if the force gathers along the arcs of the ribs on both sides to the middle of the back (T5, Lingtai).

Then the trapezius drops away and only the very middle of the back draws in distinctly.

On the out-breath, just set the energy gathered in the back straight down into the exact middle of the chest (below the solar plexus).

3. Then go down into the abdomen (Mingmen ↔ lower dantian).

If you come down into the abdomen after first releasing the upper bolt and the sympathetic tension with the breastbone like this, there is no need to force the belly up and down.

On the in-breath the heat of Mingmen at the back crosses to the lower dantian, and on the out-breath it returns to Mingmen: a deep circulation continues by itself, without tension.

In short: the ribcage release that opens at the front (the sternum) and gathers at the back (Lingtai) comes first, and abdominal breathing comes after.

It is not forcing the belly to inflate; only when you empty the top first does the bottom become deep.
