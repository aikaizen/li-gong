[calm, unhurried, contemplative teacher reading a meditation text] How to cultivate the unmoving mind. A post from November 21. [break] To cultivate an unmoving mind that is not shaken by emotion, you first have to go through an autonomic spillover, in which both sides of the autonomic nervous system are activated.

1. Ordinarily, the body's arousal system (the sympathetic nervous system) and quiescence system (the parasympathetic nervous system) inhibit each other like a "seesaw": when one goes up, the other goes down.

But when deep meditation or intense breathing or ritual pushes one system up to its critical point, the inhibition releases and energy floods over into the opposite system. This spillover switches both nervous systems on at full intensity at the same time.

[break] Neurotheology divides the routes to this point into two.

Quiescence (parasympathetic) to arousal (sympathetic) spillover (the still-meditation path). When stillness and no-mind, meditative absorption, are pushed to their deepest extreme and the parasympathetic system reaches saturation, the clear arousal circuit of the sympathetic system suddenly switches on as well.

A state opens in which you are extremely, lucidly awake without falling asleep (arousal), yet without a speck of agitation (quiescence).

Arousal (sympathetic) to quiescence (parasympathetic) spillover (the dynamic-practice path). When the sympathetic system is driven to its limit, as with the strong breathing and breath retention of Tibetan tummo, the whirling dance of the Sufis, or fierce mantras, then at the moment it crosses the critical point the parasympathetic system rebounds explosively and fires at the same time.

This is the path on which, amid overwhelming energy and heat, emotion is cut off, and an unbelievable stillness and an ecstatic bliss (Active Bliss) burst forth.

2. d'Aquili and Newberg explain that when the simultaneous activation of the sympathetic and parasympathetic systems peaks, deafferentation occurs: the sensory signals going into the brain's posterior superior parietal lobe (the Orientation Association Area) are blocked.

This region is the one that normally draws the line between "where my body ends" and "where the outside world begins."

When, in that taut state of simultaneous autonomic activation, the space-time and body-boundary signals coming into this region are cut off, the brain can no longer draw the boundary between self and world.

As a result, the solid frame called "I" disappears and one passes into a state that feels like oneness with the universe or the void: the experience neurotheology calls "Absolute Unitary Being" (AUB).

3. In breastbone-circulation practice, this process is described as follows: the spillover from activating both the sympathetic and parasympathetic systems makes the chest feel taut, as if a sheer wall had been thrown up, and later that iron wall collapses.

- Third dhyāna (3), the "iron wall": the strong arousal energy of Mingmen (sympathetic) and the relaxation of rib purification and the "Om" vibration (parasympathetic) face off tautly in the center of the chest. In this stretch the simultaneous autonomic activation (spillover) is held at high density. Because the prefrontal cortex and parietal lobe still maintain a subtle boundary (subtle intentional awareness), it is felt as a solid "wall."

[calm, unhurried, contemplative teacher reading a meditation text] [break] At this point you are not shaken by any emotion, and it feels as though emotion has been cut off.

- Fourth dhyāna, the "collapse of the iron wall" (emptiness) and the "bright nature": this is the moment the taut standoff passes its critical point and the frame of the parietal and prefrontal lobes that was holding the boundary suddenly lets go (sensory deafferentation, a shift into non-doing).

The empty spaciousness where the wall has vanished (no-mind) and clear, wakeful arousal (sympathetic) coexist without conflict, and this leads into what neurotheology calls unconditioned peace and joy (the bright nature).

At this point, when the cardiopulmonary rhythm becomes extremely harmonious, the heart secretes atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP), which directly suppresses the stress hormone (cortisol) and the anxiety of the amygdala, and the oxytocin pathway is activated.

This is not dopamine-driven pleasure from being excited by outside stimuli. It is a quiet, clear joy, the joy of meditation, that wells up softly and unconditionally from deep inside the chest.
