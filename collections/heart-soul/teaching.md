## 1. Feeling your heartbeat sharpens intuition
*Nov 14, 2026 · [original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2121536288356798663)* · [audio](audio/01-2121536288356798663.mp3)

The more accurately people sense their own heartbeat, the faster and more sharply they catch the subtle warning signals and intuitions (gut feeling) that the body fires off first in systole, before the head reasons things through.

People who usually feel their heartbeat clearly in the chest nerves, the fingertips or Mixin read the tiny "catches" or "modulations" of this systolic wave immediately. In experiments with actual financial traders or seasoned field commanders, the groups that count their own heartbeats more accurately detect uncertain danger signals far earlier and show markedly better performance at avoiding losses.

This is because the baroreceptor signal that rises in systole pre-heats the superior colliculus of the midbrain and the amygdala at the same time, so that, although fine form analysis is switched off, intuitive outline-capture and the instant ignition of the muscles (saccades, evasive maneuvers), which decide life and death, evolved to happen explosively in systole.

To use the warnings of systole properly, the baseline noise has to be low. On a lake with ripples you cannot make out the ring from even a small falling stone, but on a lake as still as a mirror, the brush of a single leaf reveals the direction and size of the wave exactly.

## 2. Every heartbeat refreshes the self
*Nov 14, 2026 · [original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2121568586980557002)* · [audio](audio/02-2121568586980557002.mp3)

According to brain science (Tallon-Baudry's team and others), the brain receives the heartbeat-evoked potential (HEP) that comes up with every beat of the heart in the ventromedial prefrontal cortex (vmPFC) and the precuneus, and renews (refreshes) every second the first-person sense of self (selfhood): "ah, the 'I' who feels and thinks right now is here."

With every beat, the heart sends the information called "I" and updates it.

When the heartbeat slows and softens, the impact of this "self-refresh pulse" thins, and without straining to empty yourself, you slide naturally into no-mind (무심 無心), where the boundary between subject (I) and object blurs.

## 4. The heart's little brain and the awakening of the heart-soul
*Nov 22, 2026 · [original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2124315987566817741)* · [audio](audio/04-2124315987566817741.mp3)

The heart is not a simple blood pump. It is a "little brain" holding about 40,000 neurons, and an endocrine organ that produces calm.

1. The physiological view: the ascending circuit of the right atrium, the cardiac nerves and the vagus

Hormone secretion in the wall of the right atrium: when you release the trapezius and breathe in deep and slow, venous blood fills the heart gently and the wall of the right atrium expands.

At this point the muscle cells of the right atrium secrete ANP (atrial natriuretic peptide) and oxytocin, which directly suppress the anxiety response of the amygdala and the stress hormone (cortisol).

The heart's little brain (ICNS) and the ascending vagus: the ganglionated plexus at the back of the heart (ICNS) integrates this stable rhythm and sends it up to the brain.

About 80% of the vagus nerve linking heart and brain is not the brain's command line but a sensory pathway running up from the heart to the brain. Only when this signal of stability reaches the hypothalamus and brainstem does clear joy and calm (central oxytocin, serotonin, endorphins) spread through the whole body.

The trap of direct focus: if you pin awareness sharply on the right atrium or the heart muscle itself, the brain over-monitors the heartbeat, and instead the sympathetic nerves switch on and palpitations or tightness easily arise.

2. The meaning for practice: the awakening of the heart-soul (심혼; the Former Heaven soul of the heart)

The opening of this independent intelligence of the heart is expressed as the awakening of the heart-soul (심혼).

The way it is awakened is exquisite neurophysiologically too. It does not aim at the physical heart with force.

First you lay down an unshakable "ease" in the center of the chest, and then, like a mother hen sitting on her eggs, you resonate with the heart and wait with a "warm longing" free of impatience.

Placing longing (the most powerful emotional trigger of oxytocin) on top of ease (ventral vagal activation) is a precise design for opening the heart's little brain without touching the sympathetic alarm switch.

When the noise of the cerebrum (the discriminating mind) settles and the heart's ascending circuit opens wide, the heart becomes the "master oscillator" of the whole body's autonomic rhythm and begins tuning, in a chain, the energy of the remaining five organs as well.

The more you try to control the body with the head, the more the chest closes. Only when you open the space of the ribcage with the breath and lay a quiet longing on top of ease does the intelligence of the heart awaken.

## 5. Feel the right atrium fill: the awakening of the heart-soul
*Nov 24, 2026 · [original post](https://x.com/z9_s8_y_9/status/2125129006152589574)* · [audio](audio/05-2125129006152589574.mp3)

Required reading for anyone who, on the in-breath, only draws in air or strains to push the diaphragm down.

Not downward: as the ribcage opens, you should feel the heart, the right ventricle. 👇

If you gently open the front ribcage, a slight negative pressure forms inside the chest (the thoracic cavity), and venous blood from below is drawn smoothly up into the heart's right atrium.

It is not the force of sucking hard through a straw but a "sense of passive intake," filling naturally as space opens.

When you release the tension of the outer muscles and begin to feel this rhythmic wave of intake, the body's relaxation switches turn on one after another.

Cardiac endocrine action: the pressure receptors in the wall of the right atrium are stimulated, and the ANP and cardiac oxytocin mechanisms that relax the blood vessels and sympathetic nerves start working.

Activation of the brain's parasympathetic nerves: this signal travels up the vagus to the brain (hypothalamus) and wakes the parasympathetic circuit; a warm relief spreads in the chest and distracting thoughts settle by themselves.

If you ride the breath not as a muscle exercise of pushing out only the belly but as a flow in which the heart gently receives the blood and energy from below, the brain goes into deep stillness without forced effort.

Meditation method: pause for 1–2 seconds at the end of the in-breath and sense the "warm intake (venous blood)" in the right atrium.

When the breath has filled softly to only 70–80%, don't hold it by force; just rest lightly for about 1–2 seconds.

At this point rest awareness quietly just inside the right border of the sternum.

Check whether a subtle sense of vacuum, of something being drawn smoothly up from below (the solar plexus, the abdomen) into that spot of the right atrium, a cool yet warm filling, or a gentle pulse brushes by.

This is described as "the heart-soul (심혼 心魂) awakening."
