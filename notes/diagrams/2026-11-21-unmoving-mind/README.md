## Translation (2026-11-21)

To cultivate an unmoving mind that is not shaken by emotion, you first have to go through an autonomic spillover (자율신경 범람), in which both sides of the autonomic nervous system are activated.

1. Ordinarily, the body's arousal system (각성계, the sympathetic nervous system) and quiescence system (고요계, the parasympathetic nervous system) inhibit each other like a "seesaw": when one goes up, the other goes down.

But when deep meditation or intense breathing or ritual pushes one system up to its critical point (임계점), the inhibition releases and energy floods over into the opposite system. This spillover switches both nervous systems on at full intensity at the same time.

Neurotheology divides the routes to this point into two.

**Quiescence (parasympathetic) → arousal (sympathetic) spillover (the still-meditation path).** When stillness and no-mind (무심 無心), meditative absorption (선정 禪定), are pushed to their deepest extreme and the parasympathetic system reaches saturation, the clear arousal circuit of the sympathetic system suddenly switches on as well.

A state opens in which you are extremely, lucidly awake without falling asleep (arousal), yet without a speck of agitation (quiescence).

**Arousal (sympathetic) → quiescence (parasympathetic) spillover (the dynamic-practice path).** When the sympathetic system is driven to its limit, as with the strong breathing and breath retention (지식 止息) of Tibetan tummo, the whirling dance of the Sufis, or fierce mantras (진언 眞言), then at the moment it crosses the critical point the parasympathetic system rebounds explosively and fires at the same time.

This is the path on which, amid overwhelming energy and heat, emotion is cut off, and an unbelievable stillness and an ecstatic bliss (Active Bliss) burst forth.

2. d'Aquili and Newberg explain that when the simultaneous activation of the sympathetic and parasympathetic systems peaks, deafferentation (구심성 차단) occurs: the sensory signals going into the brain's posterior superior parietal lobe (the Orientation Association Area, 방향연합영역) are blocked.

This region is the one that normally draws the line between "where my body ends" and "where the outside world begins."

When, in that taut state of simultaneous autonomic activation, the space-time and body-boundary signals coming into this region are cut off, the brain can no longer draw the boundary between self and world.

As a result, the solid frame called "I" disappears and one passes into a state that feels like oneness with the universe or the void: the experience neurotheology calls "Absolute Unitary Being" (AUB, 절대적 단일 존재).

3. In breastbone-circulation practice (가슴뼈 순환 수행), this process is described as follows: the spillover from activating both the sympathetic and parasympathetic systems makes the chest feel taut, as if a sheer wall had been thrown up, and later that iron wall collapses.

- **Third dhyāna (3선정 三禪定), the "iron wall" (철벽 鐵壁):** the strong arousal energy of Mingmen (명문 命門) (sympathetic) and the relaxation of rib purification (갈비뼈 순화) and the "Om" vibration (parasympathetic) face off tautly in the center of the chest. In this stretch the simultaneous autonomic activation (spillover) is held at high density. Because the prefrontal cortex and parietal lobe still maintain a subtle boundary (subtle intentional awareness, 미세적 유위각 微細的 有爲覺), it is felt as a solid "wall."

  At this point you are not shaken by any emotion, and it feels as though emotion has been cut off.

- **Fourth dhyāna, the "collapse of the iron wall" (철벽 붕괴, emptiness 텅 빔) and the "bright nature" (밝은 성품):** this is the moment the taut standoff passes its critical point and the frame of the parietal and prefrontal lobes that was holding the boundary suddenly lets go (sensory deafferentation, a shift into non-doing, 무위 無爲).

  The empty spaciousness where the wall has vanished (no-mind) and clear, wakeful arousal (sympathetic) coexist without conflict, and this leads into what neurotheology calls unconditioned peace and joy (the bright nature).

  At this point, when the cardiopulmonary rhythm becomes extremely harmonious, the heart secretes atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP), which directly suppresses the stress hormone (cortisol) and the anxiety of the amygdala, and the oxytocin pathway is activated.

  This is not dopamine-driven pleasure from being excited by outside stimuli. It is a quiet, clear joy, the joy of meditation (선열 禪悅), that wells up softly and unconditionally from deep inside the chest.


## Quoted post (2026-11-18): rib purification

Usually, when emotion surges, people say, "Breathe in deeply and send it down to the lower belly." But if you force the breath down while the chest (the middle dantian 중단전 中丹田 and the ribs) and the organs (장부 臟腑) have not been purified, side effects arise.

The passion lodged in the chest (heat, sympathetic arousal) is pressed hard from top to bottom without ever being resolved, so instead the belly gets bloated, or the pressure rebounds and shoots up into the head: the rising-qi (상기 上氣) phenomenon.

As a result, the emotional wave that arose in the front of the chest easily gets trapped as it is in the intercostal nerves and rib fascia on both sides, and stiffens into a dull ache.

Rather than sending it down, try breastbone purification.

Instead of stamping emotion down, you open a horizontal rotating circuit that crosses the cylinder of the ribcage from the front (the center, 중심) to the back (Lingtai 영대 靈臺), and refine the energy.

The congestion, the density of passion, lodged in the front of the chest (the center) rides the in-breath as the ribcage widens, travels along the outer edges of the ribs on both sides, and is scattered to Lingtai at the back (around the 5th thoracic vertebra).

Because this spreads emotional energy that was clumped in one spot and about to explode across the broad surface of the left and right ribs, and drains it out through the spinal line at the back, the chest opens up refreshingly at once, without suppression.

On the out-breath, the qi gathered at Lingtai at the back (around T5, where sympathetic ganglia are dense and passion is stored) is drawn back into the center through the straight midline passage, together with the out-breath (and the sounding of "Om").

As this round trip repeats, the emotional turbid qi (탁기 濁氣) wedged in the ribs and the coarse heat of the sympathetic system are washed out, and a firm stillness that outside stimuli cannot shake (the iron wall of no-mind → breaking through → the arising of heart-fire) settles in the center.

When the front-to-back rotation of the ribcage (rib purification) is complete in this way, the ground of the chest is emptied like the void and a warm heart-fire (심화 心火) arises. Then, without any straining to send it down, that soft energy flows down by itself, like the law of gravity, to the lower belly (the lower dantian 하단전 下丹田, the Yellow Court 황정 黃庭).
